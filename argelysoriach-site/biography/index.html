Argelys Samuel Oriach

About

Argelys Samuel Oriach is a Brooklyn-based performance artist, artistic director, and systems architect known for blending performance, design, and structural innovation across media. He constructs language for the room, page, stage, and screen. As founder and artistic director at Wild Lake Studio, he scouts and develops multidisciplinary artists whose practices, like his own, span multiple forms. His central offering to artists is his undivided attention.

Born in the Dominican Republic, he was raised by a housekeeper and a facility maintainer, and learned in adulthood that his mother was a writer and his father a musician, though neither pursued their craft. His structural impulse emerged at age eleven when he taught himself 3D design software to draft his parents' dream home. An architect later used that exact file for actual construction. And in the wake of the 2008 financial crash, he applied to Phillips Academy Andover to consciously design the conditions of his own life.

A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he spent the next sixteen years treating his body, voice, and daily life as a single structural instrument. In 2010, he attended Northwestern University’s National High School Institute (Cherubs) theatre program, playing Nick in a stage adaptation of The Handmaid’s Tale. From there, he studied the mechanics of somatic weight, physical posture, and human attention by fully inhabiting roles across elite institutions, physical staging, and corporate infrastructure. He went on to obtain a bachelor’s degree in English from Wesleyan University, where he took extensive coursework in studio art and critical theory.

That foundation fueled a twelve-year trajectory building organic growth systems. During that period, he served as Cynthia Nixon’s social media manager for her 2018 campaign for governor of New York, later pivoting to architect sovereign digital frameworks from first principles. But he approached his corporate roles as an undercover durational performance. It was a calculated effort to build his life out of poverty while auditing how complex systems scale from the inside out. That performance cycle recently closed with an intentional return to his body, breath, and voice.

He operates two laboratories: Seven Eighty Three (Lab 783), a venture foundry that births infrastructure and whole companies, and Wild Lake Studio (Wild-Lake), the research laboratory created within it, which guides developing and independent artists. Over fifteen years, he guided twelve or more developing and independent multidisciplinary artists, providing the structural stewardship, physical carrying capacity, and live production architecture that formalized into Wild-Lake.

As a writer, he applies this same somatic and structural rigor to narrative through a maximalist, automatic, and lyrical stream-of-consciousness style. He authored the short story This Is Not a Love Story and is currently writing a pilot, a one-act play, and his debut autofiction novel, Graduation. The story is a digital short written as a screenplay in prose form.

Oriach’s focus remains on the underlying physical and digital mechanics across every medium. He is still building his life out of poverty. He lives and works in New York.
